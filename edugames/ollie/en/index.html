What are Ollie's Games

Ollie's Games are three free learning games for children in kindergarten and first grade (about 5 to 8 years old). Ollie the owl guides them, praises them and reads to them out loud. The games run in the browser on iPad, tablets, phones and computers: nothing to install, no sign-up and no ads.

They were made for practising at home with a first grader and work well in the classroom too. Buttons are big enough for small fingers, the text uses a font made for beginning readers, and every game has settings for grown-ups.

Ollie's Classroom – English for kids

An interactive game for English words and simple sentences about school. Ollie speaks English and asks questions such as “What's this?” or “What colour is the paint?”. The child picks a picture or an answer and then says it out loud.

Five lessons: classroom things (crayon, pencil, glue, paint, book, paper), colours and numbers, old and new (ruler, rubber, schoolbag), the letters a, b, c and tidying up (“Put the crayon in the pencil case, please.”). Czech hints can be switched on or off, and correct answers earn stars.

Ollie's Numbers – adding and taking away

Addition and subtraction practice for first graders. Sums are made up as you go, the child taps the answer on a big number keypad, and Ollie reads each sum in Czech or English. After a correct answer Ollie reads the whole sum, for example “Three plus two is five”.

Grown-ups set the top number (10 by default, up to 999) and choose plus, minus or both. Easy level finds the answer (3 + 2 = ?), hard level finds the missing number (? + 2 = 5). Coloured dots help with small numbers, the score shows at the bottom, and a run of correct answers earns stars.

Ollie Reads – first reading in English

A first-reading game based on the Learn to Read 1 (My Pets) and Learn to Read 2 (At the Park) books, with 20 extra words and phrases such as “a squirrel at the park” or “Venice in Italy”. It also practises sight words: a, see, is, for, me, at, the, an, in.

Five activities grown-ups can pick from: read and find the picture, picture to word, listen and find the word, make the sentence, and read out loud. Tap any word to hear it. Printable cards let you practise away from the screen.

Playing on an iPad

Open the page in Safari and tap Share → “Add to Home Screen”. The games then open full screen, and the “Games” button in the corner brings the child back here. Turn the sound on, because Ollie talks.

Progress and settings are saved only on the device the child plays on.
